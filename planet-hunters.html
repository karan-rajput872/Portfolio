Section B

Project Overview

Transforming space telescope photometric time-series into actionable astrophysical intelligence using robust feature engineering and heterogeneous gradient boosting.

What the Project Accomplishes

Modern space telescopes capture millions of photometric measurements per observation sector. Genuine planetary transits cause subtle, recurring geometric dips (< 1% flux attenuation) as an orbiting planet temporarily blocks a fraction of its host star's light.

This project implements a fully autonomous Machine Learning pipeline for the Hack4Hack 2026 ML Challenge to distinguish true exoplanet signals from stellar phenomena (eclipsing binaries, starspots, pulsators, energetic flares) and spacecraft detector artifacts (momentum dumps, thermal breathing, pointing jitter).

Why Classifying Stellar Observations is Challenging

  • •
    Severe Signal-to-Noise Ratio: Transit signals are shallow and frequently drowned out by rotational starspot waves or Gaussian photon shot noise.
  • •
    Astrophysical Mimics: Unresolved background eclipsing binaries blended within the same telescope aperture generate false-positive dips nearly identical to transit depths.
  • •
    Detector Systematic Drift: Spacecraft momentum dumps and camera/CCD thermal expansions induce artificial trends that cause baseline models to hallucinate pulsations and glitches.

Astro-Cotrending Innovation: Mitigating Hardware Distribution Drift

Reported -60.3% Drift Reduction

Space telescopes observe stellar fields across distinct optical cameras and CCD sensor channels. Spacecraft maneuvers (e.g. reaction wheel desaturation, momentum dumps) and orbital thermal breathing induce common-mode systematic flux fluctuations and pixel pointing jitter. If left uncorrected, machine learning models erroneously confuse these instrumental fluctuations with intrinsic astrophysical variability—causing baseline models to hallucinate pulsators and glitches across nearly half the test sector.

1. Camera & CCD Grouping

Stellar observations are grouped by physical optical channel: (camera, ccd). All stars on the same physical sensor share synchronous orbital temperature and pointing conditions.

2. Common-Mode Flux Removal

Computes the instantaneous median flux across all stars in each channel at every cadence, subtracting the shared systematic trend to unmask true stellar variability.

3. Common-Mode Centroid Vectoring

Subtracts common-mode 2D centroid drift vectors, isolating true astrophysical astrometric displacement from spacecraft pointing drift.

// Conceptual Astro-Cotrending Workflow Architecture:
Raw Light Curves (Flux, Centroid X, Y) + Metadata (Camera, CCD) │ ├──> Group Observations by [Camera, CCD] Channel │ ├──> Compute Channel Median Flux per Cadence: M_flux(t) = median_{stars}(Flux_i(t)) │ └──> Compute Channel Centroid Jitter: M_cx(t) = median(CentroidX_i(t)), M_cy(t) = median(CentroidY_i(t)) │ ├──> Common-Mode Detrending & Outlier Clipping: │ ├──> Flux_cotrended(t) = Flux_raw(t) - M_flux(t) │ └──> Centroid_cotrended(t) = Centroid_raw(t) - [M_cx(t), M_cy(t)] │ └──> Outcome: Train-to-Test KS Feature Drift reduced by 60.3%* | Hardware Generalization: 0.5161 ➔ 0.6336 Macro F1
Scientific Integrity Note on Drift Reduction

The reported 60.3% reduction in Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) train-to-test feature distribution drift represents an experiment-specific reported result on this competition dataset and evaluation configuration, rather than a universal scientific guarantee across arbitrary astronomical instruments.

88 Domain-Engineered Astrophysical Features

Tailored feature extraction designed around physical stellar mechanics and photometric signal geometry.

5 Core Feature Groups

1. Box Least Squares (BLS) Transit SNR

Periodically folds the cotrended flux time-series across grid search periods to identify periodic, flat-bottomed geometric dips. Computes transit depth, duration, period, and statistical signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) to distinguish true periodic exoplanets and eclipsing binaries from non-periodic stellar noise.

2. Astrometric Centroid In-Dip Shift

Measures 2D pixel displacement of the photometric center-of-light during flux minima versus out-of-dip baseline. Important scientific methodology note: Centroid-shift analysis is employed as a method of helping identify possible background blended binary contamination (where light from a faint background binary shifts the aperture centroid during an eclipse); it serves as a critical candidate vetting filter, rather than definitive proof of planetary origin.

3. Flare Rise-Decay Asymmetry

Analyzes impulsive flux surge profiles by computing the ratio between rapid flux rise time and gradual exponential thermal cooling decay time, isolating explosive magnetic reconnection events in active M-dwarf flare stars.

4. Fourier Power Harmonics

Evaluates Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) spectral power density, prominent peak frequencies, and spectral entropy ratios. Disentangles rapid multi-mode acoustic oscillations in pulsating stars from multi-day quasi-sinusoidal rotational starspot modulations.

5. Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) & Scale Moments

Computes robust non-parametric statistical dispersion metrics including Median Absolute Deviation (MAD), interquartile range (IQR), skewness, kurtosis, and rolling quantile ratios. These provide scale-invariant baselines resilient against single-cadence cosmic ray hits, separating photometrically quiet stars from instrumental anomalies.

Distinction: Cross-Validation Performance vs. Hardware Generalisation

Standard 5-fold cross-validation evaluates performance across unseen stellar observations sampled randomly from the training distribution. Out-of-detector generalisation, in contrast, explicitly holds out entire camera and CCD channels from training. Because each optical channel possesses unique pointing jitter profiles and thermal drift curves, evaluating on held-out detector hardware tests true physical invariance. Both metrics are genuine offline evaluation results—strictly documented as offline benchmarks without claiming public leaderboard rankings or competitor comparisons.
